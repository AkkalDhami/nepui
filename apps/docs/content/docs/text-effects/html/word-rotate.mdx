## Usage

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="tokens.css" />
<link rel="stylesheet" href="word-rotate.css" />
```
```html
<p>
  Build interfaces that feel
  <span class="np-word-rotate" role="img" aria-label="fast, calm, sharp, alive">
    <span class="np-word-rotate-item" aria-hidden="true">fast</span>
    <span class="np-word-rotate-item" aria-hidden="true">calm</span>
    <span class="np-word-rotate-item" aria-hidden="true">sharp</span>
    <span class="np-word-rotate-item" aria-hidden="true">alive</span>
  </span>
</p>
```

No script is needed. Every `.np-word-rotate` works with `[data-np-word-rotate]`, and every `.np-word-rotate-item` works with `[data-np-word-rotate-item]`.

Use up to 10 words. The rotator is as wide as its widest word, so it keeps the rest of the sentence from shifting while the words change. Words of a similar length look best in the middle of a sentence.

---

## Variants

`data-variant` sets how a word enters and leaves: `fade`, `flip`, `blur` or `zoom`. Leave it out for `slide`.

## Speed and direction

`data-speed="slow"` keeps each word twice as long as the default and `data-speed="fast"` half as long. `data-direction="reverse"` moves words the other way. `data-animate="false"` shows the first word and stops.

## Inline

<ComponentPreview
  name="word-rotate-inline"
  target="html"
/>

When a word is narrower than the widest one, use `data-align` to choose where it sits: `center` or `end`. The default is the start. Use `center` when the sentence itself is centered.

---

## Accessibility

- Put `role="img"` and an `aria-label` that lists every word on the root, and `aria-hidden="true"` on each word. A screen reader then reads the full set once and does not announce each change.
- The words are real text. They stay selectable and can be translated.
- With reduced motion turned on, nothing moves and the first word stays on screen. Do not put information that a reader must not miss only in the later words.
